Natsume

棗 — Tea Caddy

A lacquered caddy for thin-tea matcha, named for its jujube-fruit silhouette.

Core utensilLacquerwareFor thin tea

Name & Types 名前と種類

NameNatsume 棗 ("jujube")
SizesLarge (ō-natsume), medium, small
FinishesPlain black shin-nuri, tame-nuri, maki-e gold designs, mother-of-pearl

History 歴史

The natsume is a Japanese invention, attributed to the Muromachi-era lacquerer Haneda Gorō. Against the ceramic chaire used for thick tea, the light lacquered natsume became the standard container for thin tea.

From Rikyū's preferred plain black to later caddies painted with the four seasons in gold maki-e, the palm-sized vessel concentrates the essence of Japanese lacquer craft.

Parts 部位の名称

  1. 1
    Futa / kō (lid & crown)
    The top face — the showpiece for maki-e
  2. 2
    Aikuchi (joint)
    Where lid meets body; precision here is the mark of quality
  3. 3
    Dō (body)
    The gently swelling side
  4. 4
    Soko (base)
    May carry the maker's signature

How to Use 使い方

1

Handling

Rest it on the left palm and purify the lid with the fukusa in the prescribed strokes.

2

Filling

Mound the matcha softly like a small mountain; sift it just before use.

3

Care

Never wash with water. Wipe with a soft dry cloth and store in its wooden box.

Buying Secondhand 中古で選ぶポイント

Lacquer condition

Look for cracks, flaking and dulling. Sun-fading cannot be reversed.

Fit of the lid

A fine caddy's lid settles with a gentle sigh. Rattling or looseness is a warning.

Maki-e wear

Gold designs wear with use — zoom in on photos to confirm the outlines survive.

Box & maker

A signed box, and origins such as Wajima or Yamanaka, guide the price.
