Hishaku

柄杓 — Water Ladle

A bamboo ladle for hot and cold water. Its single, silent pour is one of the beauties of the procedure.

Core utensilBamboo craftRo & furo

Name & Types 名前と種類

NameHishaku 柄杓 ("ladle")
By seasonRo: larger cup, end cut on the inner face / Furo: smaller cup, cut on the outer face
NoteGarden (tsukubai) ladles are a different item

History 歴史

Water ladles are ancient ritual objects, but tea refined the bamboo hishaku into a centerpiece of the procedure, with dimensions that change between hearth and brazier seasons.

A fresh white-bamboo ladle is itself hospitality — its clean pallor honors the guest, and long use turns the cup a warm amber.

Parts 部位の名称

  1. 1
    Gō (cup)
    The vessel that holds the water
  2. 2
    Tsukigata
    The crescent cut where cup meets handle
  3. 3
    E (handle)
    The long stem with its fixed node position
  4. 4
    Kiridome (end cut)
    Bevelled opposite ways for ro and furo

How to Use 使い方

1

The stance

Held in the "mirror ladle" pose, as if reflecting one's own heart.

2

Drawing & pouring

Pour in a single thread; what remains never returns to the kettle.

3

Hiki-bishaku

In the brazier season, the ladle is drawn back with extended fingers — a famously graceful move.

Buying Secondhand 中古で選ぶポイント

New for use

Cleanliness comes first at tea; treat secondhand ladles as practice or display pieces.

Cracks & warp

Dry storage splits the cup; check the handle for bowing.

Ro or furo

Identify by the end cut and cup size, and match your season of practice.

Sashi-tōshi

One-piece cup-and-handle ladles rank highest; check joint precision otherwise.
