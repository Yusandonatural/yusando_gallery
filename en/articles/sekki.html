READING — SEASON

The 24 Solar Terms & Tea Utensils

The tea year turns with the old solar calendar. From Risshun to Daikan, a term-by-term guide to seasonal arrangements.

The Turning Year 一年のめぐり

The 24 solar terms divide the year by the sun's path, and the tea room turns with them. There is no single correct pairing — but anticipating the season by half a step is the host's art. Here is the classic year, term by term.

SPRING — 春

立春

RISSHUN — spring begins

RO

c. Feb 4

Spring on the calendar. February's grand hearth and the last of the New Year willow; camellia and a plum branch for the alcove.

雨水

USUI — snow to rain

RO

c. Feb 19

Snow softens to rain. Dolls' festival motifs: shell-pair kōgō, peach-blossom bowls.

啓蟄

KEICHITSU — insects wake

RO

c. Mar 6

The hanging kettle lifts the fire from the floor; spring-field motifs on the bowl.

春分

SHUNBUN — spring equinox

RO

c. Mar 21

Equinox week. With Rikyū's memorial at month's end, a quiet, wabi arrangement suits.

清明

SEIMEI — clear and bright

RO

c. Apr 5

Full cherry blossom. The sukigi kettle hides the fire; let sakura bowls and caddies bloom.

穀雨

KOKUU — grain rains

LAST RO

c. Apr 20

Rains for the seedlings. The hearth season closes, thoughts turn to the 88th-night new tea.

SUMMER — 夏

立夏

RIKKA — summer begins

FURO

c. May 6

First furo: the whole room refitted. Green maple motifs, half a year from the jar-opening.

小満

SHŌMAN — all things fill

FURO

c. May 21

New-tea season. Young-leaf motifs and a green bamboo lid rest, all freshness.

芒種

BŌSHU — grains sown

FURO

c. Jun 6

The rains arrive. Hydrangea and firefly motifs; utensils that enjoy the sound of rain.

夏至

GESHI — summer solstice

FURO

c. Jun 21

Longest day. A shallow bowl and a glass water jar — coolness for the eyes.

小暑

SHŌSHO — lesser heat

FURO

c. Jul 7

Tanabata. Mulberry-leaf lid trick (habuta) and star-festival motifs; morning tea begins.

大暑

TAISHO — greater heat

FURO

c. Jul 23

Peak heat. The famous-water and wrung-cloth procedures serve coolness itself.

AUTUMN — 秋

立秋

RISSHŪN — autumn begins

FURO

c. Aug 8

Autumn on the calendar. Slip one autumn-grass motif in among the lingering heat.

処暑

SHOSHO — heat retreats

FURO

c. Aug 23

Insect voices. Musashino-plain motifs; pampas grass in gold on the caddy.

白露

HAKURO — white dew

FURO

c. Sep 8

Dew glints white. Moon motifs take the lead, earthy bowls carry the season.

秋分

SHŪBUN — autumn equinox

NAKAOKI

c. Sep 23

Harvest-moon weeks. The brazier moves to the mat's center (nakaoki), edging the fire toward the guests.

寒露

KANRO — cold dew

NAKAOKI

c. Oct 8

Dew turns cold. Nakaoki continues, with motifs of fruit and turning leaves.

霜降

SŌKŌ — first frost

NAGORI

c. Oct 23

The furo's farewell. A worn brazier and humble utensils — the year's most wabi tea.

WINTER — 冬

立冬

RITTŌ — winter begins

RO

c. Nov 7

Robiraki and the jar-opening: the tea person's New Year, toasted with sweet zenzai.

小雪

SHŌSETSU — light snow

RO

c. Nov 22

First cold showers. Glossy-leaf and first-snow motifs; an Oribe shoe-shaped bowl shines.

大雪

TAISETSU — heavy snow

RO

c. Dec 7

Deep winter, short days. Toward evening gatherings (yobanashi) by lamplight.

冬至

TŌJI — winter solstice

RO

c. Dec 22

The sun turns. A yuzu kōgō wishes health; light returns from here.

小寒

SHŌKAN — lesser cold

RO

c. Jan 6

Into the cold — and hatsugama, the first kettle: knotted willow and festive stacked bowls.

大寒

DAIKAN — greater cold

RO

c. Jan 20

Coldest of all. A cylinder bowl and the wrung hot cloth hand warmth, steam and all, to the guest.
